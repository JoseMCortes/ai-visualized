Training a Word Embedding, Step by Step

Every word starts as a random dot. The algorithm walks through a tiny corpus and, one tiny nudge at a time, pulls words that appear together closer and pushes unrelated words apart. After a few hundred nudges the words sort themselves into groups. Step through it, or press Play.

This is skip-gram with negative sampling — the core of word2vec — but shrunk for clarity: a ~30-word corpus and 2-dimensional vectors so the map is the model. Real embeddings use billions of words and hundreds of dimensions; the arrows on the map are exaggerated so a nudge is visible.

How to read this

  1. A word is just two numbers — its position on the map. That pair of numbers is the entire "vector" for the word; training only ever changes these numbers.
  2. 1 — pick a pair. A center word and one of its real neighbours in the text (highlighted in the corpus below).
  3. 2 — score it. Multiply the two words' numbers pairwise and add them up (the dot product), then squash that to a 0–1 value with sigmoid. For the real neighbour we want 1; for a few random "negative" words we want 0. The gap is the error.
  4. 3 — nudge. Adjust both numbers of each word a little to shrink that error: −(learning rate) × error × (the other word's numbers). For the real neighbour this makes the two vectors point more the same way (dot product up), which usually pulls the dots closer — green. For the negatives it does the opposite — red.
  5. Repeat for thousands of pairs. No one defines "animal" or "food" — the groups appear because those words keep the same company.

Word2Vec, on Real Text

Pick a book. The demo pulls out its most frequent content words, starts each as a random point, and runs skip-gram — nudging words that appear near each other together, pushing random pairs apart. Press Play and watch the words drift into groups by the company they keep. Click any word to see its nearest neighbours.

Same algorithm as the step-by-step demo, on a real corpus. The vectors are 16-dimensional (2-D collapses on real text); the map is a PCA view of them, rotated to stay steady, and coloured by cluster. A single book is tiny next to what real word2vec trains on, so expect suggestive groupings, not perfect ones.

How to read this

  1. The words — the most frequent non-stop-words in the text (list on the right, with counts). Stop-words like "the" and "and" are dropped; very common words are thinned so they don't dominate.
  2. Each word is 16 numbers, not 2. When two words appear near each other, all 16 of each word's numbers get nudged a little so the two lists line up better (their dot product grows); a few random words get nudged to line up less. Same rule as the step-by-step demo, just with 16 numbers instead of 2.
  3. The map is a flat 2-D shadow of those 16 numbers (PCA), so two dots sliding together on screen is really their 16-number vectors becoming more aligned. k-means colours the emerging clusters.
  4. Click a word to pin it and see its nearest neighbours by cosine similarity — that's the actual output of a word embedding.

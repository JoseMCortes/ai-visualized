Training an Attention Head

Self-attention has three learned matrices — W_Q, W_K, W_V — and training nudges all three by gradient descent, the same as any other layer. This demo trains one real attention head on a 3-token, 3-dimensional toy example small enough to watch every number: "the cat sat", with "sat" as the query, learning to pull in information from its subject, "cat".

Every matrix here is real linear algebra, not illustration — forward pass, MSE loss, hand-derived backprop, and a learning-rate-0.5 update, checked line by line against the PyTorch snippet below (every gradient matches PyTorch's autograd to the last printed digit). What's not real: a single training example repeated forever, one head instead of many in parallel, 3 dimensions instead of hundreds, and a target vector handed directly rather than arriving indirectly through next-token loss several layers later. The mechanics — forward, loss, gradients, update — are exactly what you see here.

How to read this

  1. Q, K, V are just three learned lenses on the same embeddings. q = x · W_Q asks "what am I looking for?" (only the query token needs one); K = X · W_K and V = X · W_V give every token a "what do I offer as a match?" and "what do I actually hand over?".
  2. The √3 in the scores is the same scaling real transformers use — without it, scores grow with dimension and softmax saturates into a near one-hot distribution regardless of what the matrices have learned.
  3. The backward pass sends the error at the output back through the softmax and both linear projections. W_V's gradient is usually the largest, since values feed the output directly with no softmax in between softening it — watch which matrix the step narration names as the biggest mover; it isn't always W_V.
  4. A note on what "learning" looks like here: after a few steps the loss collapses to nearly zero, but attention doesn't fully swing onto "cat" — "sat" keeps attending to itself the most, even once training has converged. Most of the actual fix comes from W_V reshaping the values themselves, not from attention isolating the subject. That's a real, checked property of this small example, not a simplification.
  5. Part 2 reuses the exact same forward() — nothing special-cased — on a brand-new sentence, with the last word as the query and the matrices frozen wherever training currently stands.

Ranking Documents for RAG

When an AI app answers from your documents, step one is finding the right ones — that's a ranking problem, and there is more than one way to solve it. Below: the same 18 short documents and the same handful of queries, ranked six different ways. Pick a method, pick a query, and watch a document's score get built up piece by piece.

Every method here is implemented from scratch — no external search index and no real language model. The two "trained" methods (cross-encoder, learning to rank) learn from just four hand-labelled example queries, which is enough to show the mechanism but far too little to generalize the way a production system — trained on thousands of examples — would.

How to read this

  1. The document list (left) always shows the same 18 documents, re-ranked by whichever method is selected. The dots on the right of each row are this demo's own answer key — how relevant that document actually is to the query — so you can see how closely a method's ordering matches it.
  2. Click any document to see exactly how its score was computed, on the right. For TF-IDF and BM25, press Play to watch the score build up one query word at a time.
  3. TF-IDF → BM25 → embeddings → hybrid is a natural progression: word counting, a more careful version of word counting, meaning instead of exact words, then a blend of both.
  4. Cross-encoder and learning to rank start untrained — every score is 0 until you press Step, Play, or Run to train them on this demo's four labelled example queries, live, from scratch.
